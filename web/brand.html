unit-safe serialization.

Bovnar's identity says the same thing the format does: every value travels wrapped in its validated type and unit. The mark is a measured value, held in its annotation — nothing assumed, nothing ambiguous.

01 — The Mark

A value, held in its annotation.

The mark fuses Bovnar's two defining glyphs. The blue brackets are the type annotation — <float:64,m/s> — drawn as caliper jaws that measure and grip. The teal gem is the value itself: faceted, exact, never bare. Together they read as a single idea — a measurement that carries its own meaning.

‹ ›  annotation  ·  ◆ value

03 — Clear Space & Sizing

Give it room to be read.

Keep clear space equal to the width of the gem (one ◆) on every side. Never place type, rules, or other marks inside that boundary.

Below ~24px the full caliper mark loses its end-ticks — switch to the simplified favicon glyph (chevrons + gem), which holds its silhouette down to 16px.

06 — Voice

Precise, plainspoken, unhyped.

"A bare 9.81 is never ambiguous, and a mismatched unit is a parse error."
PRIMARY
Unit-safe serialization for scientific & industrial systems.
SHORT
Every value carries its unit.
TECHNICAL
Self-describing, dimensionally trusted, no external schema.
Voice — do
  • State the failure mode plainly: the wrong unit is the bug.
  • Show the syntax; let <float:64,m/s> do the talking.
  • Speak to engineers — telemetry, instrumentation, control.
  • Be exact about what Bovnar does and doesn't do.
Voice — don't
  • No "revolutionary", "next-gen", or "AI-powered" framing.
  • Don't claim unit conversion — Bovnar validates, apps convert.
  • Avoid emoji and exclamation in technical copy.
  • Never imply a schema or compiler step is involved.
